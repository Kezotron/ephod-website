About EPHOD

EPHOD stands for Every Person, Household, Oikos — Discipled. Oikos is the Greek word for a household and the circle of people around it. The vision behind the name is that every person, every family and every circle of friends becomes a follower and disciple of Jesus Christ.

EPHOD is not a book or a course you finish. It is a way of living with the Bible: reading it every morning and every evening, hiding a verse in your heart each week, writing down what God shows you, and meeting with a few other believers to share it. Do that for a year and you have read the whole Bible — and you have become someone who can help the next person do the same.

The program was developed by Rev. Herb and Rev. Karen Johnson during thirty years of missionary ministry with the Assemblies of God in the Philippines and Papua New Guinea. Today it is carried forward by the churches they served, on this website, and in the EPHOD apps.

Where EPHOD came from

The Johnsons were appointed Assemblies of God missionaries in 1974. Wherever they served, they kept meeting the same need: believers who loved Jesus and wanted to grow, and pastors who had no practical way to train them. Ephesians 4:11–12 says leaders are given to the church "to equip the saints for works of service," and the Johnsons set out to build something a pastor anywhere could use to do exactly that.

It took more than ten years. What came out of it is the EPHOD Church Strengthening Seminars — thirteen seminars that cover nearly every foundational principle of the Christian life, built around one conviction: a disciple is someone who continues in God's Word (John 8:31) and goes to make other disciples (Matthew 28:19). The seminars have been taught to hundreds of people in Cambodia, the Philippines and Papua New Guinea, in city churches and in highland villages with no electricity or running water. In Papua New Guinea, churches filled for the whole seminar, night after night.

Because it was shaped across so many cultures, EPHOD travels well. It does not depend on a building, a budget or a seminary. An EPHOD lifestyle just needs a Bible, a notebook, a few fellow believers and a decision to keep living for Jesus every day.

The 10 Point Plan

The seminars turn discipleship into ten habits, and the habits are the heart of EPHOD:

  1. Daily Bible reading
  2. Weekly Scripture memorization
  3. Sunday school and worship
  4. A weekly care cell, small group or Bible study
  5. A devotional journal
  6. Pray and fast one meal each week
  7. Sharing or witnessing Jesus
  8. Tithes and offerings
  9. Daily prayer and worship time
  10. Taking notes in services

What EPHOD gives you every day

The daily reading

An Old Testament passage each morning and a New Testament passage each evening. Follow it from January to December and you have read the whole Bible. The prophets are read in the order they lived, and the New Testament is arranged so the story keeps coming back to the life, death and resurrection of Jesus. The plan is the Full Life Study Bible Reading Plan, used by permission of Life Publishers International.

Small groups

EPHOD today

In Papua New Guinea

The program the Johnsons carried into the highlands now continues under its own leadership as EPHOD of Papua New Guinea, within the Assemblies of God of Papua New Guinea. The EPHOD PNG app serves believers there and Papua New Guineans in Australia and the Solomon Islands, in English and Tok Pisin, with Scripture in more than two hundred Papua New Guinea languages so that people can read the daily passage in their own tokples — their heart language.

In the Philippines

Binondo Full Gospel Church in Manila keeps the weekly rhythm going: the memory verse and the devotion are chosen and posted there every week. The EPHOD PH app speaks English, Tagalog and Chinese, and it was the young people of the church who asked for Scripture in the modern Tagalog they use every day.

The apps

EPHOD PH and EPHOD PNG were built by Kevin Johnson, Herb and Karen's son and the missions coordinator at Victorville First Assembly of God, so that the reading plan could live on a phone. He launched EPHOD PNG in Papua New Guinea in person in 2026. Many believers there walked to a top-up store and bought a day's worth of data just to download the app; some had come from villages where the signal carries only calls and texts. That is why the apps work entirely offline once the Bible is on the phone, and why they are free, with no ads and nothing to buy.
